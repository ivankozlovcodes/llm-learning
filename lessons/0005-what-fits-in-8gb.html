What Fits in 8 GB

The machine in your bag, not the one on your desk. Four lessons of theory arrive at one uncomfortable number — and it is smaller than the models you picked.

~15 minutes Prerequisites: 1, 3 Mission · Glossary · Cheat sheet

Everything so far has been sized against a 64 GB machine. The laptop you actually have with you has 8 GB, and that is not a smaller version of the same problem — it is a different problem, where the KV cache stops being a line item and becomes a third of the budget.

The one idea

On an 8 GB Mac your real budget is about 4.2 GB, not 8. macOS takes the rest, and unlike a discrete GPU there is no separate pool to fall back on — the operating system and the model are competing for the same bytes.

Everything below follows from that one number.

A number you have seen before. 68.25 GB/s is, to within a rounding error, the Jetson Orin Nano's 68 GB/s. Your travel laptop and the $249 edge device sit in the same bandwidth class. The Mac wins on usable memory and on not needing a separate host — but the token rates you can expect are the same order.

The 27B at "1-bit" is the furthest from fitting of anything on the list — its weights alone exceed the whole budget, and its cache is the largest per token of any model here. Two intuitions collided: 1-bit sounds tiny and 27B is only three times 9B. Neither survives contact with the arithmetic.

The name is not the number

UD-IQ1_S is 1.81 effective bits per weight, not 1. Derived: the BF16 files total 54.7 GB, so at 2 bytes per parameter the model is 27.3B parameters; 6.19 GB × 8 ÷ 27.3B = 1.81. Lesson 6 is entirely about why the label and the number differ, and what you give up down there.

The context gap

Qwen3-4B advertises a 262,144-token context window. On this machine, after 2.50 GB of weights, you have ~1.7 GB left. At 144 KB per token:

1.7 GB ÷ 144 KB per token = ~12,000 tokens 4.6% of the advertised window

This is the same shape as the Orin Nano's 12k in Lesson 4, and it is not a defect in either machine. Context windows are a property of the model; how much of one you can hold is a property of your memory. The two are advertised together and have almost nothing to do with each other.

What going over actually looks like

Exceeding the budget does not produce an error. macOS compresses memory, then swaps to SSD, and the model keeps running — at a fraction of the speed. Lesson 1's rule tells you why: your effective bandwidth stops being 68 GB/s and becomes SSD bandwidth, which is one to two orders of magnitude lower.

SituationEffective decodeHow it feels
Qwen3-4B Q4_K_M, fits~18 tok/sFaster than you read

The diagnostic: if a model that should hit the ceiling from Lesson 1 is running an order of magnitude below it, you are not underpowered, you are over budget. Check memory pressure before you blame the hardware or the quantization.

Three levers when it does not fit

1. Quantize the cache, not just the weights

--cache-type-k q8_0 --cache-type-v q8_0 halves the cache. On Qwen3-4B that takes 16k of context from 2.42 GB to 1.21 GB — the difference between 8k and 16k of usable context for free. On a memory-starved machine this is the highest-value flag there is.

2. Cap the context deliberately

Most runtimes allocate the cache for the context size you declare, up front. Asking for 262k on this machine fails at load time or silently reserves memory you cannot spare. Set it to what you will actually use — 8k or 16k — and the budget stops being a mystery.

3. Prefer a smaller model at higher precision

Qwen3-8B at Q3 (4.31 GB) and Qwen3-4B at Q8 (4.28 GB) are the same size and neither fits. But between 4B at Q4 and 8B at Q2 at around 2.5 GB, the smaller-model-higher-precision side generally wins — and Lesson 6 shows you the curve that explains why.

Check yourself

Question 1

Your Mac has 8 GB of unified memory. Roughly how much can a model actually use?

About four. macOS plus a browser and terminal takes 3–3.5 GB, and unified memory means there is no separate pool to spill into. That 4.2 GB has to cover weights and the KV cache together — which is the constraint most model recommendations quietly ignore.
Question 2

A 6.19 GB model loads on an 8 GB machine and runs at 0.4 tok/s. What is happening?

Swapping. The bandwidth rule predicts 68 ÷ 6.19 ≈ 11 tok/s ceiling. Running 25× below that means the bytes are no longer coming from RAM — effective bandwidth has become SSD bandwidth. No error is raised, which is exactly why this gets misdiagnosed.
Question 3

Qwen3-4B advertises a 262k context window. How much fits on this machine after weights?

~12,000 tokens, under 5% of the advertised window. The context window is a property of the model; how much of it you can hold is a property of your memory. They are printed on the same spec sheet and are almost unrelated.
Question 4

Memory-starved and needing more context. Which single change buys the most?

Quantize the KV cache. Q8 halves it at negligible quality cost, and at long context the cache is the part that is growing. Dropping weight precision instead costs quality on every token to solve a problem that only appears at length — the wrong trade for the symptom.

Where this goes next

Twice now the answer has been "use a lower quantization" — and twice I have waved at a quality cost without pricing it. That is the gap Lesson 6 closes: what the quant names actually mean, where the quality curve bends, why IQ1_S is 1.81 bits rather than 1, and whether a bigger model squeezed harder beats a smaller model treated gently.

Addendum · the 48 GB M4 Added 2026-09-05 — the lesson above stands as written; this is what a third machine changes.

A 48 GB M4 MacBook Pro now travels alongside the 8 GB M1. Its practical budget is ~36 GB — roughly 75% of installed memory, the same convention Lesson 3 uses for the 64 GB machine. That is 8.6× this lesson's 4.2 GB, and it changes the answer to every question on this page.

The lesson's central tension disappears. On 4.2 GB the KV cache is a third of the budget and every context setting is a fight. On the M4, Ornith's 80 KB/token buys 178,625 tokens of cache from the 14.29 GB left over — more context than the model can use well. The constraint stops being memory and becomes something else entirely, which is what Lesson 18's addendum is about.

Two things survive intact. The method — weights plus KV against a budget — is unchanged; only the budget moved. And the 8 GB machine is still in the bag, so every number above the fold still applies on the days you use it. Full arithmetic in Three Machines.
