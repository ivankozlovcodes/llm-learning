Three Machines

A 48 GB M4 joins the trip. Every budget in this workspace was written for two machines; here is the third, and the short list of conclusions it overturns.

Added 2026-09-05 Amends 5 · 12 · 16 · 17 · 18 · 19 Napkin Math · Glossary
Check this before trusting any number below

Apple sells 48 GB on two different chips, and they differ by 2× in the specification that governs decode speed:

M4 Pro — 273 GB/s. 12-core CPU / 16-core GPU, or 14-core / 20-core.
M4 Max (16-core CPU, 40-core GPU) — 546 GB/s.

Run sysctl -n machdep.cpu.brand_string and check the GPU core count in → About This Mac. If it says Max with 40 GPU cores, it is the fastest machine you own — faster than the 64 GB M1 Max at home. If it says Pro, it is about two-thirds of the home machine's bandwidth. Configurations and bandwidth from Apple's MacBook Pro (14-inch, M4 Pro or M4 Max) tech specs: "273GB/s memory bandwidth" for both M4 Pro tiers; "410GB/s" for the 14-core M4 Max and "546GB/s" for the 16-core; 48 GB is offered on "M4 Pro and M4 Max with 16-core CPU".

What 36 GB unlocks

The travel machine becomes a real machine. Ornith-1.5-35B-A3B at 21.71 GB fits with 14.29 GB to spare — that is Lesson 15's recommendation for the 64 GB machine, now in your bag. So does Qwen3.8-27B (16.46), Qwen3-30B-A3B (~18.7) and Qwen3 32B (~20.1). Llama 3.3 70B at 42.5 GB still does not.
Two models resident at once. Ornith (21.71) plus the WeMM-Embedding-9B embedder with its vision tower (6.252) is 27.96 GB, leaving 8.04 GB — about 100,000 tokens of KV cache at 80 KB/token. Generation and retrieval, simultaneously, on a laptop. Neither fits alone on the 8 GB machine.
Real fine-tuning. A full fine-tune of Qwen3-1.7B costs 19.60 GB and now fits — no LoRA required. QLoRA on a 4B at batch 4 and 2048 tokens comes to about 14 GB, so batch size stops being pinned at 1.
The binding constraint flips. On the 8 GB machine memory binds at ~11,800 tokens. On the M4 with Ornith the memory ceiling is 178,625 tokens and the attention ceiling is ~32,000 — so attention binds, by 5.6×, exactly as it does on the home machine. The failure mode changes from loud to silent.

What does not change

The 8 GB machine still exists. It is still in the bag, and every 4.2 GB conclusion still applies when you are using it. Nothing in Lessons 5, 16, 17, 18 or 19 was wrong; a fourth column was added to the table.
Tokenization, ordering, the cache rule. Lesson 17's chars-per-token measurements, Lesson 18's stable→volatile ordering, Lesson 2's byte-identical prefix rule — all machine-independent. More memory buys none of them.
Attention degradation. 36 GB of KV cache does not make a model reliable over 178,000 tokens. Context rot is a property of the model, not the machine, and the extra memory makes it easier to walk into.

One caution about the corp machine

It is not yours. Disk encryption, MDM inventory agents and DLP tooling all sit between you and a 22 GB model file, and some of them will scan it every time it is read. Keep models under your own home directory, expect the first load after a reboot to be slow, and check whether your employer's policy covers downloading multi-gigabyte model weights before you fill the disk with them. Nothing in this workspace requires you to put anything confidential into a model.
